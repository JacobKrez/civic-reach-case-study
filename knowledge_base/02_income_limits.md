# FNS / SNAP Federal Poverty Guidelines (Gross Income Test)

## Federal Gross Income Standard — 130% of Poverty
Most households (without an elderly or disabled member) must have **gross
monthly income at or below 130% of the federal poverty guideline** for their
household size to pass the initial FNS/SNAP income screen.

The federal poverty guidelines are published each year by the U.S. Department
of Health and Human Services (HHS). USDA FNS updates its SNAP eligibility
charts on its website after each annual release. Always check the current
year's chart before deciding eligibility.

based on each year's guidelines. North Carolina follows the federal chart.)

## Net Income Standard — 100% of Poverty
Households that include an elderly member (age 60 or older) or a disabled
member are **not subject to the gross income test**. They must instead pass
the **net income test**, which compares net monthly income (after allowable
deductions) to **100% of the federal poverty guideline** for the household
size.

## Expanded Eligibility for Most Households
Under the federal Fiscal Responsibility Act and updated FNS guidance,
households subject to the gross income test whose gross income is **above
130%** but **at or below 200% of poverty** may still be eligible if they
pass the net income test and all other rules. This expands access for many
working families and is reflected in NC's screening logic.

## Verification
Income limits change every federal fiscal year. Before giving a final
eligibility answer, the agent should:
1. Note which year's guidelines are in effect.
2. Direct the user to confirm current figures on the USDA FNS eligibility
   page: https://www.fns.usda.gov/snap/eligibility-income-and-deductions

## Important Note for North Carolina
NC FNS uses the federal gross and net income limits without state-level
adjustments to the percentages. The household size counts everyone who lives
together and purchases and prepares food together, with a few exceptions
covered in the household composition document.
