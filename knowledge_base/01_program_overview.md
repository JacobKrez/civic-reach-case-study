# NC Food and Nutrition Services (FNS) — Program Overview

## What FNS Is
Food and Nutrition Services (FNS) is North Carolina's name for the federal
Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). It is administered by the
NC Department of Health and Human Services (NCDHHS), Division of Social
Services, under federal rules from the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)
Food and Nutrition Service (FNS).

## Who FNS Serves
FNS helps low-income households buy the food they need for good health.
Eligibility is based on income, household size, and a small number of
non-financial rules (citizenship/qualified non-citizen status, work
registration for able-bodied adults, residency in North Carolina).

## How Benefits Work
- Benefits are issued electronically on an EBT (Electronic Benefits Transfer)
  card.
- The card works like a debit card at most grocery stores and many farmers
  markets.
- Benefit amounts are based on the federal Thrifty Food Plan and the
  household's net monthly income after deductions. The maximum allotments
  are updated each October by USDA.

## Application Paths in NC
Applicants can apply:
- Online at epass.nc.gov (the state's common portal for DHHS benefits).
- In person at the local county Department of Social Services (DSS) office.
- By mail or fax using a paper application.

An interview (phone or in person) is required before eligibility is decided.

## Recertification
Eligibility is reviewed at regular intervals (typically every 6 or 12 months,
depending on the household). The county DSS sends a recertification form
that must be returned by the deadline or benefits stop.

## Where to Get Help
- Local county DSS office (directory on the ncdhhs.gov county services page).
