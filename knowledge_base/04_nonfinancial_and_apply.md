# Non-Financial Eligibility Scope and How to Apply

## Volatile rules are outside this screen
Federal work-requirement and non-citizen eligibility rules changed materially
in 2025. FoodPath does **not** evaluate either topic and does not ask for a
person's immigration status. A county Department of Social Services (DSS)
office must evaluate the current rules and any individual exemptions or
documentation. Do not use an income-screen result from FoodPath as an
opinion about eligibility under these rules.

For a current determination, apply through ePASS or contact the applicant's
county DSS office. A person may apply for other household members without
asking FoodPath to evaluate anyone's immigration status.

## Residency and identity
Applicants seeking NC FNS generally apply through the county where they live.
The county DSS determines what identity and residency verification is needed.
FoodPath does not collect identity documents, Social Security numbers,
addresses, or immigration documents.

## How to apply in North Carolina

1. **Online:** use the official ePASS portal linked from the NCDHHS FNS page.
2. **In person:** visit the local county DSS office listed by NCDHHS.
3. **By mail or fax:** request the current paper application and submission
   instructions from the county DSS office.

The county DSS schedules any required interview, requests verification,
decides eligibility, and explains appeal rights. Processing time and expedited
service depend on current agency rules and the household's circumstances;
confirm them with DSS rather than relying on this screen.

## Expedited (7-day) service eligibility

W43 close-the-loop addition. SNAP regulations at 7 CFR §273.2(i) require
state agencies to provide expedited service to households in certain
categories so that benefits are issued within 7 calendar days of the
application date. The three federal categorical triggers are:

  1. **Very low income and resources.** Households with gross monthly
     income under $150 and liquid resources (cash, checking, savings)
     of $100 or less.
  2. **Migrant or seasonal farm worker.** Households with a migrant or
     seasonal farm worker who is currently not receiving income from
     that work.
  3. **Destitution / housing-cost crisis.** Households whose combined
     monthly gross income AND liquid resources are less than the
     household's monthly rent/mortgage plus utilities, OR whose
     current rent/mortgage plus utilities exceed a documented
     threshold (DSS validates the figures).

The actual determination is made by the county DSS office during the
application interview — not by this screen. The fastest path to an
expedited determination is to apply through ePASS or at the county
DSS office and explicitly request expedited processing. The agency
must verify the categorical trigger before issuing benefits on the
7-day timeline.

## Preparing for DSS
DSS may request proof concerning identity, household composition, income,
and allowable expenses. Ask DSS which documents apply before sending
sensitive records. FoodPath never needs those records to run its limited
income screen.

## Documents DSS may ask for (W43 close-the-loop KB addition)

DSS verifies eligibility based on documents the applicant supplies. The
exact list depends on the household's circumstances; ask DSS which
documents apply before sending sensitive records. The most common
categories are:

  - **Identity / residency.** A photo ID (driver's license, state ID,
    passport) and proof of NC address (utility bill, lease, mail).
  - **Household composition.** Names, dates of birth, and relationships
    of every person in the household. For each person, Social Security
    numbers if available (FoodPath does not collect these).
  - **Income.** Recent pay stubs (typically the last 30 days), a letter
    from the employer, or self-employment records. For each source of
    income (wages, Social Security, unemployment, child support,
    pensions, etc.), DSS may ask for a verification letter or stub.
  - **Allowable expenses (only if the household has an elderly or
    disabled member).** Out-of-pocket medical receipts, dependent-care
    receipts, current rent/mortgage statement, utilities bills.

DSS may also ask for immigration documents for any household member the
applicant chooses to include; this screen does not ask and does not
evaluate immigration status. For a current list and per-county guidance,
contact the county DSS office; FoodPath never sees these documents.

## If the application is denied
Read the denial notice for the stated reason, deadline, and instructions for
requesting a fair hearing. Contact county DSS for help understanding the
agency's current process; FoodPath does not provide legal advice.

Fair-hearing requests have a strict deadline (typically 90 days from the
denial date, but confirm on the notice itself). The hearing is conducted
by the NC Office of Administrative Hearings; DSS cannot be present at
the hearing without the applicant's consent. Legal aid is available in
NC for SNAP fair-hearing representation — see
https://www.nclegalaid.org for current intake.
